# Cellular Respiration (study note)

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to release
energy stored as ATP. In eukaryotes it happens mostly in the mitochondria and
occurs in three main stages.

## 1. Glycolysis
Glycolysis takes place in the cytoplasm and does not require oxygen. One molecule
of glucose (a 6-carbon sugar) is split into two molecules of pyruvate (3 carbons
each). The net yield is 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose molecule.

## 2. The Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle)
The Krebs cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. Each pyruvate is first
converted to acetyl-CoA, releasing carbon dioxide. Per glucose, the cycle produces
2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH2, and releases 4 molecules of CO2 in total.

## 3. The electron transport chain
The electron transport chain is located on the inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH
and FADH2 donate electrons, and oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor, forming
water. This stage produces the majority of the cell's ATP — roughly 34 ATP per
glucose molecule.

## Summary numbers
- Total ATP yield per glucose: about 38 (often cited as ~30–32 net in practice).
- Reactants: glucose + oxygen.
- Products: carbon dioxide + water + ATP.
- Overall equation: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy.
